TIMELINE • ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

THE HISTORY OF AI

From fictional ideas about intelligent machines to systems that can learn, reason, and assist with complex tasks, AI has developed through centuries of ideas and decades of research.

1726

THE IDEA OF MACHINE INTELLIGENCE

Jonathan Swift's Gulliver's Travels described a fictional machine called the Engine that combined words to help scholars generate ideas and writings. It was an early example of imagining machines assisting intellectual work.

MILESTONE 01
1912–1914

THE FIRST CHESS AUTOMATON

Spanish engineer Leonardo Torres Quevedo built El Ajedrecista, an electromechanical machine that could automatically play a simplified chess endgame. It was built in 1912 and demonstrated publicly in 1914.

MILESTONE 02
1951

MACHINES BEGIN TO LEARN

Marvin Minsky and Dean Edmonds built SNARC, one of the earliest artificial neural networks. It explored how machines could learn through feedback and reinforcement.

MILESTONE 03
1955–1959

AI BECOMES A FIELD

The 1955 Dartmouth proposal introduced the term "artificial intelligence," and the 1956 workshop helped establish AI as a research field. The Perceptron and Arthur Samuel's checkers program soon advanced neural networks and machine learning.

MILESTONE 04
1990s–TODAY

FROM RESEARCH TO EVERYDAY LIFE

AI advanced through greater computing power, data, and machine learning. Deep Blue defeated Garry Kasparov in 1997, AlexNet advanced image recognition in 2012, AlphaGo defeated a top Go player in 2016, and ChatGPT brought generative AI to a massive public audience in 2022.

MILESTONE 05
END OF TIMELINE

WHERE DO WE GO NEXT?

AI has evolved from imagined mechanical intelligence into systems capable of learning, creating, and assisting with complex tasks. Its history gives us a starting point for thinking about where AI could go next.
